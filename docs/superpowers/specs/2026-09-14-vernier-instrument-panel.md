# Vernier — the instrument-panel build spec

Status: approved design, generated by a 17-agent workflow (3 directions, 3 judge lenses each, 1 synthesis).
Date: 2026-09-14

## Directions considered

**Vernier** — 6.7/10

The screen is an instrument panel, not a page. Everything a PHC actually runs on is a calibrated artifact — the token slip, the OPD card, the ruled vitals chart, the lab worksheet — and every one of them is a grid with values landing on it and a printed identifier down the left margin. So: a matte green-grey panel ground (not paper, not cream), a single Dial surface that holds all content, one 4px/20px measuring grid taken from ECG paper (1mm small square, 5mm large square) that governs every edge in the app, numerals set in a tabular mono at every size including the largest so there is no display face at all, and a perforated token stub running down the left of every subject row. Nothing decorative survives: an instrument shows a scale, a pointer, and a lamp that is dark until something is wrong.

**Stock and Rule** — 6.7/10

The product is set, not styled. Identity comes from one grotesque at five sizes, one typewriter face for every value a human reads aloud, a 4px baseline with every row height divisible by 8, and a single 1px rule that is never decorative — between queue rows the rule IS the waiting clock. Two materials: stock (the tinted ground everything sits on) and field (true white, which appears only where a person enters or reads a record). Colour is one hue with two stops and it encodes elapsed time against a declared target, nothing else. Red is absent from the entire product on purpose: the monitors in the room already own red under IEC 60601-1-8, and a UI that spends it on a selected nav row degrades the real signal. Every measured alignment defect is structural, so every fix is structural — one grid owns the topbar and the page, one container owns the rail's inset, one token owns a card's padding. There is nowhere left to type a different number.

**Waterline** — 6.3/10

A PHC is not a dashboard, it is a queue with a clock on it. So the organising variable is not category, role, station or bundle — it is ELAPSED TIME AGAINST A DECLARED LIMIT. Every group on screen owns one clock and one hairline; the hairline fills left-to-right as that group's oldest item consumes its limit. Nothing is coloured for what it is. A thing takes colour only when its clock has overrun, and loses it the instant a human touches it. That single mechanic replaces status chips, severity dots, badge counts, toasts, modals and the entire "needs attention" vocabulary. The screen is silent on a good morning and legible from four metres away on a bad one, and the difference is a LENGTH before it is a hue — which is why it survives colour-blindness (7.5–11% of Indian males), greyscale printing, and peripheral vision from behind a counter.

## Direction chosen and grafts

VERNIER is the spine: the screen is an instrument panel, not a page — a matte panel ground, one lit surface, a 4/20 measuring grid governing every edge, numerals in a tabular readout face at every size (the largest type on any screen is a number, never a headline), and a perforated token stub ruling the left margin of every list. Home becomes a single-column rack of full-width plates; the sidebar becomes two states (264px or gone); colour is a law, not a palette.

The five weaknesses the judges raised against Vernier are fixed, not accepted:

(1) "IBM Plex Mono is itself the category's second-tier default." FIXED by swapping both faces for Atkinson Hyperlegible Next + Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono (Braille Institute, SIL OFL, Google Fonts). The argument is safety, not fashion: the failure this product must never have is two patients confused, and the only widely-available family engineered for character disambiguation (tailed l, open-tailed J, asymmetric b/d/p/q, a 0 that cannot be an O) is the right face for a 14-digit ABHA read aloud at a counter. It is also not Inter, Geist, Söhne or Plex, so the resting screenshot is not the house style it was accused of.

(2) "Act red is declared and never sited." FIXED: red now has a defined trigger (2x the declared target, or a declared escalate_minutes), exactly five render sites (the row's overrun bar, the row's age cell with the words "act now", the plate header count, the pinned nav count, the collapsed toggle badge), a hard budget (one act element per plate; the rest collapse into "+N act now"), a persistence channel that survives navigation (the rail count, the collapsed badge, and "(N)" prefixed to document.title so a backgrounded tab still shows it), and one aria-live announcement per crossing. No toast, no modal, ever.

(3) "The redundancy amber depends on — sort position — is destroyed by the plate body's 2x2 auto-fill." FIXED by deletion: a plate body is always one column. Over-threshold rows sort to the top of an unambiguous vertical list.

(4) "The declared threshold does not exist in the contract." FIXED honestly: one additive contract change (ViewDecl.target_minutes, ViewDecl.escalate_minutes, QueueRow.entered_at — all optional), and the documented fallback is that a bundle declaring no target renders pure monochrome and loses nothing. The laboratory bundle ships without it on purpose, which is also the mutation proof for the colour gate.

(5) Smaller: the graticule is deleted outright (a risk whose default position was already the safe one is not a risk); the live ticking clock is deleted (it is the only thing on screen that would repaint every second in a product that snaps everything else); 11px is no longer allowed to carry a value (identifiers and staleness move to 13px mono, 11px survives only for unit suffixes and transient modifier hints); ⌘⇧N and ⌘1..9 are browser-reserved on Chrome/macOS and are replaced by Alt+N and Alt+1..9; the drag handle is absolutely positioned so it is visually first and last in tab order, and contributes zero to the plate header's height; rail state is keyed by kernel AND role so a shift change on a shared terminal restores the default (expanded).

Copying Claude's sidebar interactions is not a defect to be fixed — the owner asked for it in the verbatim brief. What is NOT copied: the icon rail (Claude has none either), the hover-expand flyout (a nurse mid-vitals crosses the left edge dozens of times an hour), DOM-order tab stops, and the hover-revealed per-row overflow menu.

### Grafts accepted
- Stock and Rule — THE WAIT RULE, grafted as the colour mechanism rather than as a second signature. The 2px bar at the bottom of every subject row is that row's clock: inked fraction = elapsed / declared target, --ink up to 100%, --over past it, --act past escalation. It gives Vernier's amber and red a place to live, and it restores length-before-hue redundancy so a deuteranope, a greyscale printout and peripheral vision all still read it. Implemented as one absolutely-positioned ::after with transform:scaleX(var(--t)); transform-origin:left — one composited layer, zero layout, zero added row height.
- Stock and Rule + Waterline — THE HEIGHT-LOCKED 48px RESUME BAND replacing the greeting: 'Resume · Sunita Devi — address, half typed   [Take it]'. It never changes height (when there is nothing to resume the same 48px carries facility · date · role), so nothing below it ever moves. Interruption runs 7.9/hour and 18.5% of interrupted tasks are never resumed; a cue that is only 64% reliable still beats no cue.
- Stock and Rule + Waterline — THE REORDER MECHANISM, whole: pointer events with setPointerCapture instead of HTML5 drag-and-drop; insert index computed from sibling MIDPOINTS so forward and backward drags are symmetric; ONE move(from,to) shared by pointer and keyboard so the swap-vs-splice divergence cannot exist; FLIP on transform only; drag state in a ref instead of useState; ViewRenderer memoised so a pointer crossing does not re-render every plate body.
- Waterline — THE SELECTOR GATE: no rule may use var(--over) or var(--act) unless every selector in its list contains [data-over] or [data-act], and those attributes are set only from a declared threshold. Deleting the declared field must turn the colour off. Plus its two corollaries: colour is never the only differentiator (a word and a sort position always accompany it), and validation errors are neutral ink plus plain language, never the act hue — a clerk meets a format complaint thirty times before lunch and that is how red dies by 11am.
- Waterline — ATKINSON HYPERLEGIBLE NEXT / MONO as a character-disambiguation safety argument, replacing IBM Plex. This is the fix for the winner's worst weakness, so it is the load-bearing graft, not a cosmetic one.
- Waterline — THE COLLAPSED TOGGLE CARRIES A NUMBER, not an icon rail: a 16px badge with the worst non-zero over-threshold count. A clerk must not lose 'eleven waiting, two over' by shutting a panel.
- Stock and Rule — ZERO GLYPHS IN NAVIGATION. iconForView is deleted. The measured defect is that two of the four nav icons resolve to the same glyph (both queue and tasklist fall through to #i-list) and the third is an ECG waveform used for a dashboard, so a user who memorises the rail still cannot tell two entries apart. A 13px label is a faster discriminator than a glyph you have to learn, it is correct in every bundle for free, and deleting the mapping removes the temptation that produced the 60px mystery-meat rail. iconFor(op) stays: it is keyed to an instrument, not to a view.

### Grafts rejected
- Stock and Rule's 'no red, anywhere, ever'. Rejected: IEC 60601-1-8 reserves red for high priority because a human must act, and this product is in that building — the correct move is to reserve red so hard it is startling, not to abolish the channel. S&R's own escalation step is a darker ochre, which at 2px is indistinguishable from the ochre below it. Red stays, sited to five places and budgeted to one element per plate.
- Stock and Rule's wait rule AS THE SIGNATURE. Rejected as identity, accepted as mechanism. Two signatures is no signature; the token stub rules the left margin and the wait rule rules the bottom hairline, and only one of them is what the product is remembered by.
- Stock and Rule's Atlas Grotesk / Atlas Typewriter (Commercial Type). Rejected: unlicensed on day one, so what actually ships is Archivo + IBM Plex Mono on pale grey — precisely the default it claims to escape. Identity must not be contingent on a purchase order.
- Stock and Rule's ban on the ABDM identity blue (#2B5BD7). Rejected: it is a brand fact in a product whose subject is ABDM. It stays, contained to web/src/abha/* and asserted there — it never appears in shell, Home or thread chrome, so it cannot be confused with --hand.
- Stock and Rule's 272px rail. Rejected: 264 is already derived (264 + 40 + content + 40), and the 8px is worth more to the Thread screen's form-plus-transcript column.
- Waterline's 'colour drops the instant a human touches it'. Rejected: that builds a one-keystroke silencer into the escalation signal — open the row, the red goes away, the patient is still waiting. Colour clears when the operation that actually moves the subject commits, which resets the clock, and never on mere navigation.
- Waterline's 'Esc clears the resume band'. Rejected: Escape is a reflex key and the band holds the one piece of state a clerk cannot rebuild from memory. It clears on an explicit dismiss control or on completing the form.
- Waterline's 'no drop indicator, the gap is the indicator'. Partially rejected: the gap is kept (siblings FLIP out of the way), and a 2px --hand seam is drawn inside it. A gap alone is ambiguous while the pointer sits between two plates, and the seam costs one absolutely-positioned div.
- Vernier's own graticule (a visible 20px calibration grid on the canvas). Dropped. Its default position was already the safe one and its main justification was a design-QA benefit paid for in clerk seconds. layout.check.ts makes misalignment un-mergeable, which is what the graticule was really for.
- Vernier's own repeat(auto-fill,minmax(320px,1fr)) plate body at >=900px. Dropped — it destroys the sort-position redundancy the colour system depends on.
- Vernier's own ⌘⇧N primary-action shortcut and ⌘1..9 nav jumps. Dropped: both are reserved by Chrome on macOS (new incognito window; tab switching), so the highest-value pixel in the product would print a key the platform never delivers. Replaced with Alt+N and Alt+1..9, which fire in Chrome on both platforms and are suppressed while a text field has focus.

## The colour law

ONE LAW, TWO CLAUSES, BOTH MECHANICALLY ENFORCED.

CLAUSE ONE (Vernier's law): colour may only sit on a pixel that can change during a single shift. If a region would still be that colour tomorrow morning with the same data, it is neutral. Consequences, all testable: the Home nav row is never coloured for being Home (it is always Home), but the SELECTED row is, because selection moves; a station, a bundle, a role, a brand mark, a plate and a validation error are never coloured, because they are nouns and nouns do not change. The check is a screenshot: capture Home twice a week apart against the same fixture; any coloured region identical in both is a bug.

CLAUSE TWO (Waterline's gate, the enforcement mechanism): no CSS rule may use var(--over) or var(--act) unless EVERY selector in its list contains [data-over] or [data-act]. Those attributes are set in exactly one place in the renderer, from elapsed >= a DECLARED threshold. Delete the declared field and the colour must vanish everywhere — that is the mutation proof, and it is the one design.check.ts runs.

WHAT SETS THE ATTRIBUTE. elapsed = now − (row.entered_at ?? row.opened_at). target = decl.target_minutes. escalate = decl.escalate_minutes ?? 2 x target. elapsed >= target sets data-over; elapsed >= escalate sets data-act (data-act implies data-over). If target_minutes is absent — which is exactly the case for the laboratory bundle on day one — the attribute is never set and the product is pure monochrome. It loses nothing: length, sort position and the word still carry the reading.

COLOUR IS NEVER THE ONLY CHANNEL. Every [data-over] or [data-act] element carries, without exception: (a) a length — the wait rule's inked fraction; (b) a word — the age cell reads "18m over" for amber and "41m · act now" for red; (c) a position — the row sorts to the top of its plate. A bare coloured dot does not exist anywhere in this product. This is what makes it survive the 5–11% male CVD prevalence in North Indian populations, a greyscale printout, and peripheral vision from behind a counter.

WHERE --act IS ALLOWED TO APPEAR — the complete list, five sites and no sixth:
1. the overrun segment of the row's wait rule;
2. the row's own age cell, with the words "act now";
3. the plate header's act count ("2 act now"), which is also where the second and subsequent act rows collapse to — at most ONE --act element renders inside a plate body at a time;
4. the pinned nav row's count in the rail;
5. the collapsed toggle's 16px badge.
Persistence: because sites 4 and 5 are in the rail, an act item survives navigation and survives the panel being shut. document.title is prefixed "(N) " while any act item exists, so a backgrounded tab shows it. One aria-live="polite" announcement per crossing, not per poll. There is no toast and no modal in this product.

WHERE COLOUR MUST NOT GO. (a) Validation errors in registration: neutral ink plus plain language. A clerk doing 40–80 registrations meets a format complaint thirty times before lunch, and spending the act hue there is how red stops meaning anything by 11am. (b) AI authorship: labelled, never tinted — notes are read in greyscale printouts. (c) Anything encoded ONLY as colour: station, bundle, queue state, role. (d) Dense data regions: the thread body, vitals rows, table cells — except a single mark on the one out-of-range value. (e) "You are here" as a hue: selection is a --dial wash plus weight 600 plus a 2px --hand bar plus aria-current="page". The old .nv.on{background:var(--eka-blue-bg);color:var(--eka-blue)} rule is deleted.

WHAT CLEARS COLOUR. Only the operation that actually moves the subject and resets its clock. Not opening the row, not hovering it, not navigating past it. An acknowledge-to-dismiss gesture is the documented failure mode of every alert surface in the literature and it is not built here.

## Type scale

TWO FAMILIES, BOTH SIL OFL, BOTH ON GOOGLE FONTS, BOTH FROM THE BRAILLE INSTITUTE'S 2025 RELEASE.

UI — "Atkinson Hyperlegible Next", system-ui, sans-serif. Weights 400 / 500 / 600.
READOUT — "Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono", ui-monospace, monospace. Weights 400 / 500, font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining always.
CO-SCRIPT — "Noto Sans Devanagari" at 105% size and +2px leading against the Latin, so a patient's name in Devanagari does not sit visibly smaller than the same name in Latin.

The choice is a safety argument, not a taste one: the failure this product must never have is two patients confused, and these are the only widely-available families drawn specifically for character disambiguation at low acuity — tailed l, open-tailed J, asymmetric b/d/p/q bowls, a 0 that cannot be an O, a 1 that cannot be an l. Staff are 20–60 years old under 4000K tubes for eight hours; presbyopia starts at 40. The readout face carries every value a human reads back aloud — ABHA numbers, tokens, doses, BP pairs, times, ages, counts, identifiers — on the rule that if a clinician could misread it to a patient's harm, it is set in the mono. Fixed advance width is functional here: a count going 9 to 10 must not reflow a row a clerk is aiming at, and every counts column lines its units digit on one x.

THE SCALE — FIVE font-size values in the entire product, no half-pixels, every line-height divisible by 4:

  11 / 16, mono 500, +0.02em, uppercase — UNIT SUFFIXES ONLY (mmHg, °C, kg, hrs) and the Alt-held digit hints. This size may never carry a value, an identifier or a timestamp. It is the one exception to the 13px floor and it is bounded by rule.
  13 / 20, sans 400 — body, row secondary text, section headers (sentence case, --ink-2), form labels, staleness. Nav rows and emphasis at 500. This is the size the clerk lives in for eight hours.
  13 / 20, mono 500, tabular — identifiers, tokens, stub digits, ages, counts, times, "as of 14s". The old build's 11px identifier moves here: the number a clerk reads back to confirm which Sunita Devi this is does not get the smallest size in the system.
  15 / 24, −0.005em, sans 400 for a subject name in a row, 600 for a plate title and a selected rail row.
  20 / 24, mono 500, −0.01em, tabular — a plate's metric values.
  32 / 36, mono 500, −0.015em, tabular — the live scale's counts and nothing else anywhere.

RULES. No 700 anywhere — hierarchy is 400 → 500 → 600 and then space. No italic (it fails first at 13px on a glare-lit panel). No uppercase above 11px: every instance of the 9.5px/700/+0.09em tracked micro-label is deleted, since that is the single most generic dashboard signature in the current build. Acronyms upper case (ABHA, ABDM, PHC), roles sentence case. 78ch measure in the thread body; rows are unconstrained because they are not prose. Density is bought by deleting rows, never by shrinking type. The largest thing on any screen is a number — there is no display face in this product at all.

This replaces 21 sizes including 8.5, 9.5, 10.5, 12.5 and 13.5. layout.check.ts fails the build on any sixth value.

## Spacing scale

BASE 4px, MAJOR 20px. The complete legal set of px literals anywhere in web/src: 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 60, 64 — 1 and 2 permitted only in a border, a ring or a bar. Nothing else may appear in a padding, margin, gap, width, height or inset. That alone erases the 22-value spread the audit measured (every integer from 1 to 20, plus 22 and one −9px); 9px appeared 38 times and 11px 18 times, and neither survives.

RULE A — INSIDE a component every value is a multiple of 4; BETWEEN components every value is a multiple of 20.

RULE B — EXACTLY THREE HORIZONTAL INSET TOKENS, AND NO COMPONENT MAY DECLARE ITS OWN:
  --inset-rail 8px — the sidebar's only horizontal padding. Left spine x=8, right spine x=256 (264−8).
  --inset-content 40px (16px at <=900px) — the page's only horizontal padding, read by BOTH the topbar and the view.
  --inset-plate 20px (16px at <=900px) — every row inside a plate, header rows included.
Every other element sets padding-inline:0.

HOW EACH MEASURED DEFECT BECOMES UNCONSTRUCTIBLE.
  58px topbar/page disagreement (.tb-title x=266 vs .wrap x=324, agreeing only at or below 1568px): .topbar{padding:0 18px}, .view{padding:16px 20px} and .wrap{max-width:1280px} centring are deleted. Both read padding-inline:var(--inset-content) and .wrap gets max-width:none. One definition of the left edge, no centring inside a remaining column, so they agree at every viewport.
  49px card-title-vs-content offset (.card-h{padding:12px 14px} against zero-padding .mgrid/.tasklist rows): the plate is ONE grid with padding-inline:var(--inset-plate); header and body are its children and share the column. Right edges match for the same reason.
  Five sidebar insets (14/10/10/10/10), the toggle orphaned at x=201, the + detached at x=205: one .rail-body{padding-inline:var(--inset-rail)}, every descendant at 0, and margin-inline-start:auto is BANNED — the .ml-auto utility is deleted from theme.css and from every call site. Trailing chrome is the last cell of a grid-template-columns:auto 1fr auto row that already ends at the spine.
  21px orphaned indent under the live line: .flow{padding:16px 14px} and .fl-col{padding:0 7px} go with the .flat class that stopped justifying them.
  The 44x44 handle measuring 26px in layout and inflating a 42.8px header to 51px: the handle is position:absolute and has no layout box.
  .sb-add{width:26px} silently overridden to 32px by .icon-btn later in the same file: both rules are deleted; one 28x28 size token for section affordances, one 32x32 for chrome.

VERTICAL RHYTHM, five row heights only: 24 section header, 32 nav row, 40 subject/patient/primary/footer row, 44 plate header, 48 topbar and resume band, 60 live scale. Pitch = height + 4 inside a list. Gaps: 4 within a list, 20 between bands in a group, 40 between groups, 0 between plates (a 1px --rule seam instead).

ENFORCEMENT — web/src/layout.check.ts, new, in the repo's existing text-parsing idiom (a .check.ts may never import a .tsx or load a browser). It parses EVERY .css under web/src (theme.css, renderer.css, voice/*, scribe/*, abha/*) plus every px literal inside a style={{...}} in every .tsx — which is where Khoj.tsx's padding:"38px 22px" is hiding — and fails on: any px literal outside the legal set; any horizontal padding on a selector that is not one of the three inset owners; any margin-left/margin-inline-start:auto; any font-size outside the five legal values; any line-height not divisible by 4. Proved by mutation: set one nav row to padding:0 10px and it must fail; add font-size:12px and it must fail; restore .ml-auto and it must fail.

## Sidebar

EXPANDED 264px (--rail-w), --inset-rail 8, so every row is x=8 w=248 and every trailing control ends at x=256. Ground --rail #D2D7CC with a 1px --rule right edge and no shadow. The painted rail is position:fixed; an in-flow spacer of the same width owns the layout, so content never reflows during a state change.

WHAT REPLACES THE FOUR UNLABELLED ICONS: nothing. There are TWO states — 264px, or gone. .sb.collapsed{width:60px} is deleted along with every `collapsed ? undefined : name` label suppression in Shell.tsx. Collapsed = the panel is absent, main content is the full viewport width, and ONE 32x32 toggle sits at (8,8) at z-index 50 over the content, carrying a 16px badge with the worst non-zero over-threshold count across all pinned queues (--ink when no threshold is declared, --over when crossed, --act past escalation). A label and its icon live and die together: if a label cannot be shown, the row is not shown. Four unlabelled icons — two of which resolve to the same glyph because iconForView keys on v.kind and both queue and tasklist fall through to #i-list, with none of them carrying a title attribute — is not a smaller sidebar; it is recall-based navigation for a user interrupted 7.9 times an hour who fails to resume 18.5% of them. Tooltip on both states, 13px below the button, flush-left, 400ms in / 0ms out / 80ms fade: "Hide sidebar ⌘B" / "Show sidebar ⌘B". That tooltip is the only place the shortcut is discoverable.

CONTENTS, TOP TO BOTTOM.

## Card reorder

POINTER EVENTS PLUS FLIP ON TRANSFORM. Nothing else animates.

WHY HTML5 DRAG-AND-DROP IS DELETED, NOT FIXED: it never fires on touch in any browser, setDragImage is never called so the user drags a transparent 44x44 button rather than a 632px card, effectAllowed/dropEffect are unset so the cursor shows a copy badge telling the user they are duplicating, the dragenter/dragleave pair flickers on every child crossing with no enter counter, and dropOnto always splices the source BEFORE the destination — measured live as a silent no-op for every forward drag while the destination card carried class "drag-over" promising a landing. With the two cards this bundle declares, that is 50% of all possible gestures doing nothing.

THE HANDLE. A real <button type="button" aria-label={`Reorder ${title}`}>, 28x28 visual, position:absolute at left:var(--inset-plate);top:8px inside a position:relative plate header — so it has NO layout box and cannot inflate the header from the 42.8px the title needs to the measured 51px. Hit area 44x44 via ::after{position:absolute;inset:-8px}: the target grows, the layout box does not. It is LAST in DOM order inside the header (title, counts, primary action, handle) and visually first, so a keyboard-only clerk reaches the plate's real work before its cosmetic ordering. Glyph: two 12px horizontal rules 4px apart — the plate's own seam repeated, not a stock six-dot grip.

SEQUENCE, WITH DURATIONS.
  pointerdown on the handle → setPointerCapture; every plate's rect recorded once. Nothing moves until 4px of travel, so a click stays a click.
  LIFT, 0ms — the dragged plate takes position:relative; z-index:2; will-change:transform; box-shadow:0 2px 0 rgba(27,31,28,.10). No scale, no rotation, no opacity: an instrument module slides in its rack, it does not tilt or ghost, and any easing on the element under the finger is felt as lag.
  TRACK, 0ms — transform:translate3d(0,dy,0), Y only, one composited property. Reorder is 1-D because the stack is one column, which also fixes the axis bug for free: today .home-cards is a horizontal flex row while Home.tsx handles only ArrowUp/ArrowDown, so ArrowDown moves a card sideways and both horizontal arrows are dead.
  INSERT INDEX from sibling MIDPOINTS, never from the destination's index. This is the root-cause fix that makes forward and backward drags symmetric.
  SIBLINGS, FLIP, 180ms cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1) — First: getBoundingClientRect on every plate. Last: commit the new order. Invert: transform:translateY(prev−next) with transition:none inside useLayoutEffect so nothing paints at the wrong place. Play: next rAF, transform:'' with transition:transform 180ms. ONLY transform is transitioned; no layout property animates, so the metric grids and tasklists inside never re-measure mid-gesture.
  DROP INDICATOR — a 2px --hand seam drawn full content width INSIDE the gap the siblings have already opened; 90ms opacity in, 0ms out. Not a highlighted destination card: that was the actively misleading part, because the insert was always "before". A seam has no before/after ambiguity, and the open gap alone would be ambiguous while the pointer sits between two plates.
  RELEASE, 180ms — the dragged plate animates from its translate to 0 on the same curve; shadow and z-index drop at 0ms on finished; will-change cleared; focus returns to the handle.

ONE MOVE FUNCTION. move(from,to){const n=arr.slice();n.splice(to,0,n.splice(from,1)[0]);return n} — shared by pointer and keyboard. The current build has two semantics in one component (moveBy swaps with a neighbour, dropOnto removes and splices) which diverge the moment there are three plates; one function makes that bug unconstructible.

KEYBOARD TWIN, identical path. Focus the handle; ArrowUp/ArrowDown call the same move() and run the same FLIP; Home/End to the ends; Escape restores the index recorded on focus. One aria-live="polite" region announces "Needs you, position 2 of 4". The current build has no live region anywhere on the page and refocuses the moved handle, re-announcing an unchanged label, so a screen-reader user cannot tell whether the key did anything.

RE-RENDER DISCIPLINE. dragging and dragOver leave Home's useState for a ref plus a data-attribute on the stack root, and ViewRenderer is wrapped in React.memo. Today each flicker re-renders Home and, through an unmemoised ViewRenderer, every metric row and task row in every card. That is the thrash; the animation was never the slow part.

PERSISTENCE. The whole order is ONE serialised array written once on drop, not once per move, under the existing kernel-partitioned key.

WHAT SNAPS AT 0ms, everywhere in the product: row hover, pressed, selection, plate header states, count and age text changes, sidebar collapse. The only other transition anywhere is the 60ms focus ring and the 400ms linear creep of the wait rule (slow enough never to attract the eye; a threshold crossing swaps its colour at 0ms, because a colour that fades in is a colour you distrust). No spring physics, no scale transforms, no staggered entrance, no motion library.

REDUCED MOTION. @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) sets every duration above to 0ms; the FLIP still runs and simply lands in one frame. The drop seam, the pointer tracking, the handle, the keyboard path and the announcement are unchanged — reduced motion loses the interpolation, never the affordance.

## Signature — the token stub

THE TOKEN STUB. Every subject row in the product — rail patients, plate rows, thread header, Khoj results — opens with a 64px block carrying the subject's short declared identifier in 13px mono 500, right-aligned and tabular, then a 1px vertical perforation (dash 2 / gap 3, --rule, inset 8px top and bottom), then the name at 15/24, then the secondary identifier at 13px mono at the row's right spine. Every list row is 40px and every stub is 64px, so a continuous perforated left margin runs down every screen — a book of token slips torn along its edge. That is the image the product is remembered by, and no other EMR has it.

It is not ornament: the token is the only unambiguous handle at a counter where two Sunita Devis on one Tuesday is normal and picking the wrong one is a clinical error, not a navigation annoyance. Three states, no hues: waiting (plain), called (the stub inverts to an --ink block with --dial digits and the perforation goes solid 1px), done (digits drop to --ink-2). All three survive colour-vision deficiency and a greyscale printout.

64px is sized against the real data, not assumed: it holds eight glyphs of Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono at 13px (advance ~7.8px). The PHC's "A01" fits trivially and the laboratory's "25-0417" — seven glyphs, ~54.6px — fits, which is exactly the case a 44px stub measurably failed. An identifier longer than eight glyphs drops to 11px mono inside the same 64px box; it is never truncated and never ellipsised, because a truncated identifier is a safety defect.

Bundle-agnostic by construction: the renderer draws whatever the declaration names as the subject's short id, and a bundle that declares none renders the perforation alone, so the left margin still rules the page.

The wait rule — the 2px bar at each row's bottom edge whose inked length is elapsed/declared target — is deliberately NOT a second signature. It is the colour mechanism. The stub rules the left margin and is identity; the bar rules the bottom hairline and is time.
